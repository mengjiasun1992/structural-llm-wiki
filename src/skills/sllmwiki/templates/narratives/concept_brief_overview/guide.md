# Writing guide: concept brief overview

## Audience and purpose

**Takeaway:** a reader who didn't know the concept has learned it by the end.

A reader meeting the idea for the first time, who needs to know what it means, why it matters and how to tell it apart from its neighbors, so they can follow anything built on it. It stays at definition level: how a particular system implements the idea, or a subtopic worth its own explanation, is another question, named under Where to go next.

## Voice and tone

Plain words, short sentences, present tense. Neutral where sources disagree: present each view, cited.

## Structure

- **Overview:** the orientation, through the key example.
- **Definition:** the most authoritative source's definition, quoted briefly with its citation, then restated in plain words; this plain-words sentence is the one other narratives reuse, so make it stand alone. Add another source's definition only when it differs in substance; when there's no accepted definition, say so and give the one the wiki follows.
- **Key ideas:** only those a newcomer needs, each a short paragraph, in the order they build on each other.
- **Boundaries:** the line against the ideas it's confused with, and what newcomers commonly get wrong.

## Examples

| Section | The key example's part |
|---|---|
| Overview | its scene: where the reader meets the concept, and what goes wrong without it |
| Definition | the definition, in its terms, in a sentence |
| Key ideas | where each idea shows in it, a sentence each |
| Boundaries | what it would look like if it were the neighbor instead |

## Evidence

Prefer the original paper, the standard or the creator for the definition, and textbooks or encyclopedic references for the key ideas.

## Length

Short: a reader should finish it in a few minutes. Everything beyond definition level goes to Where to go next.

## Checklist

- [ ] The definition is quoted briefly, cited, and restated in a plain-words sentence that stands alone.
- [ ] Nothing goes below definition level; deeper topics are named as next questions.
