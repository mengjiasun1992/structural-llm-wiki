# Writing guide: survey

## Audience and purpose

**Takeaway:** a reader new to the domain knows its main areas, and where to go deeper in each.

A reader facing a domain or an umbrella term for the first time, who needs the lay of the land before going deep anywhere. The survey goes wide over deep: it serves the main dishes on the table, the ones a newcomer can't miss, and points to where each goes deeper. Depth on any one item is another question, named under Where to go next.

## Voice and tone

Plain words, short sentences, present tense. Even-handed across items and schools of thought; where sources disagree, give each view, cited.

## Structure

- **Scope first:** what the domain covers, where it stops, and how the items were chosen.
- **The main items:** those the most authoritative sources agree on, plus any one of them puts first. Merge items that are one idea under two names; leave out tactics that serve an item, or name them inside it.
- **At a glance:** one table, an item per row, so the reader sees the whole set before any one of them.
- **Each item briefly:** what it says, why it matters, one example, where to go deeper. The same shape for every item, and no item much longer than the others.
- **How they relate:** one diagram of the items, then a walk through it: which support each other, which trade off, which overlap.
- **Applying them,** when the domain is practical: an order to learn them in, or questions to ask of a design.

## Evidence

Prefer sources that survey the domain themselves: official principles, survey papers, textbook chapters. Cite each item to the sources that state it; an item only one source names says so. Mark your own grouping and relations as reasoning.

## Length

The budget is for breadth. When an item needs more than a few short paragraphs, it is its own question: name it under Where to go next.

## Checklist

- [ ] Scope says how the items were chosen.
- [ ] The table lists every item the body covers, and only those.
- [ ] Every item has the same shape: what, why, an example, where to go deeper.
- [ ] The diagram and the walkthrough match.
- [ ] Every term and acronym is in Terminology before its first use.
- [ ] Every claim is cited to a section, or marked.
