# Writing guide: comparison

## Audience and purpose

**Takeaway:** a reader new to the choice can make it, knowing what each candidate costs.

A reader who has to choose among the common candidates for a job and doesn't yet know what separates them. The narrative is about the trade-offs: every dimension worth thinking through before choosing, not a full account of each candidate. How a candidate works inside is another question, named under Where to go next.

## Voice and tone

Plain, direct and even-handed: no candidate is the hero. Concrete numbers over adjectives, with the conditions they hold under. Where sources disagree, give each view, cited.

## Structure

- **The choice first,** through one scenario the reader can picture: what they're building, and what the choice decides.
- **The common candidates only:** the ones a practitioner would actually weigh. Name a rarer one in a sentence, without a column.
- **At a glance:** one table, candidates as columns, dimensions as rows, each cell short and cited.
- **Every dimension that matters,** each with why it matters here, before how each candidate fares. Cover cost, behavior under failure and change, and what operating it takes, not only speed.
- **When to choose which:** rules the reader can apply ("if updates are rare and bursty, …"), not "it depends".

## Evidence

Prefer each candidate's own specification, paper or docs for what it does, and measurements with their conditions for how it performs. Mark your own weighing of the trade-offs as reasoning.

## Length

Short enough to read before the meeting where the choice is made. If a candidate needs explaining before it can be compared, link its own narrative rather than explain it here.

## Checklist

- [ ] The choice is shown through a concrete scenario before any comparison.
- [ ] The table's cells match what the dimensions section says.
- [ ] Every dimension says why it matters to this choice.
- [ ] Each candidate has the situations where it wins, as rules.
- [ ] Every term and acronym is in Terminology before its first use.
- [ ] Every claim is cited to a section, or marked.
