## 3. Overview

<The orientation, through the key example: what it is in a line, where and when one meets it, who deals with it, and why it matters.>

## 4. Definition

### 4.1 In short

<The concept in a sentence or two, concise and precise, that sets the right intuition: what kind of thing it is and what sets it apart, with its exact scope, cited; a picture of it when one helps.>

### 4.2 Precisely

<The rigorous definition from the authoritative source that defines it most exactly, quoted or framed and cited: its conditions or its formal model. Then, briefly, the subtle nuances it adds on top of the short version, and why each matters. When the field has no rigorous definition, say so, and give the criteria the wiki works with, each cited.>

## 5. Key ideas

<What follows from the definition: the few consequences a newcomer needs to use the concept correctly, one short paragraph each, in the order they build on each other.>

## 6. Boundaries (optional)

<The definition tested on the cases near its edge: which are in, which are out, and why; the neighbors it gets confused with; and what newcomers commonly get wrong.>

## 7. Where to go next (optional)

<The questions this opens, each a narrative of its own: linked when it exists, named when it doesn't.>
